## L0009 Option four

### Asked

1. "The point of this model was to try to follow what happened in the
   relevant paper. This algorithm seems to have essentially nothing to do
   with it."
2. "I don't think the multi-contact criterion is quite right. ... it's
   entirely possible to have a long lived contact even if the situation is
   not fully stable one ball rolling off of another for instance. So I think
   keeping the ability for a longish lived 'stack' of balls, but somehow
   handling their collapse. And part of it is certainly friction and spin."
3. "So yes, a design with this in mind."

### Details

Tonight is a design on paper. No code changes, no runs, no new experiments.
Clusterlifter (`method/held-contact`) is stopped as built: don't extend it,
fix it, or run it.

**The paper** is González, Risso and Soto, "Extended event driven molecular
dynamics for simulating dense granular matter" (arXiv 1012.4844), the method
`notes/contact-options.md` names for option four. Read the paper itself, not
only the summary in the notes. If you can't reach it, say so in the summary
and work from the notes.

**Item 1.** Open the design with a short table: each element of the paper's
method (the frozen state, not integrating frozen spheres, the freeze test,
slow hits on a frozen sphere as hits on an infinite mass, waking on a fast
hit, the periodic support check, storing and returning the energy removed at
freezing), and for each whether clusterlifter has it. Then say in two or
three sentences where clusterlifter left the paper (decision D0049 chose
periodic pushes with a joint solver) and what that cost.

**Items 2 and 3.** Write `lines/L0009/design.md`: a method that starts from
the paper and changes only what a vibrated layer with friction forces it to
change. For each element of the paper, say keep, change or drop, with the
reason; list anything added that the paper does not have. Every change and
addition is a named departure from the paper.

The design has to answer these, each in plain words:

- **What state a resting sphere is in.** The paper's frozen sphere does not
  move and is not integrated. Here it has to move with the plate. Say what
  is and is not computed for it.
- **Stacks that last but are not stable.** A sphere can stay in contact with
  the one below it for a long time while rolling or sliding off it. The
  design must allow such a stack to persist and must say how the upper
  sphere moves while it does. Don't require several balancing contacts
  before a sphere may rest (the Nov 16 criterion): Chris no longer thinks
  that is quite right. Don't assume one supporter makes a sphere stable
  either.
- **Collapse.** How a stack ends: what is checked, when, and what state each
  sphere is left in (position, velocity, spin) when it returns to ordinary
  collisions. Cover the plate dropping away, a supporter leaving, a sphere
  rolling off, and a fast hit.
- **Friction and spin.** The paper has neither. Say what friction does at a
  resting contact, what happens to a resting sphere's spin, and what a hit
  on a resting sphere does tangentially. Tie each rule to the base model's
  collision law.
- **Energy.** The paper stores the energy removed at freezing and returns it
  on an early wake. Say whether the design keeps that, and what the
  referee's energy audit will see at freezing, while resting, and at
  collapse.
- **Overlaps.** The engine's 2e-8 overlap check stays as it is. Show the
  design cannot create an overlap at freezing or at collapse, or say where
  it could.
- **Parameters.** Every threshold and time the design adds, the paper's
  value for it where the paper has one, and what it would be here.

Also say in a short section how the design differs from L0019 (analytical
motion, `method/ride`), which already moves held spheres with the plate.

**How to write it.** Chris could not follow `clusterlifter.md`. Define every
term where it first appears. Describe what happens to the spheres; don't use
a function, variable or constant name from the code unless you have just
said in words what it is. About two pages. End with the choices that are
Chris's to make, at most five, each stated so it can be answered from the
design alone.
